## Risks more than one member raised

1. **Unverified proof (all three).** The testimonials and the "10x faster" and "50+ brands" figures are not confirmed as coming from real customers. Agency buyers write marketing copy for a living and will spot made-up proof, and one unsupported claim makes them doubt the rest of the page.
2. **"Why not just ChatGPT with a saved brief?" (all three).** Per-client voice is the whole product. If a general AI tool with a pasted brand brief gives the same drafts, nothing is left to pay for, and an existing scheduler could add voice profiles and take the niche.
3. **Unlimited requests at an unknown cost (all three).** Every paid plan has unlimited AI requests and Agency has unlimited clients, but the cost per request is unknown. Heavy agency users could cost more than €99 a month.
4. **No acquisition channel (Offers, Monopoly).** How the first 100 customers find Coast, and what each costs, is not stated. At €19 to €99 a month there is little room to pay for marketing.
5. **The scheduler seam (Monopoly, Product).** Coast does not publish, so users copy content into another tool and update statuses by hand. The calendar risks becoming a second calendar nobody trusts, and the scheduler that owns the next step can absorb Coast's step.
6. **A 2-day trial is too short (Offers, Product).** An agency needs at least one full content cycle on real clients to judge voice quality.

## Conditions (checklist for the rest of the pack)

- [ ] Remove or verify every testimonial and figure; replace them with named agency users and a measured before-and-after, such as hours per client per week. *(founder)*
- [ ] Blind test: Coast drafts against a general AI tool with the same brief, and agency managers clearly pick Coast as on-voice. *(/founder-consumer, /founder-competitors)*
- [ ] Map who already offers multi-brand AI drafting, especially inside schedulers, and at what price; name one segment Coast can lead. *(/founder-competitors)*
- [ ] Prove a heavy user on each plan stays profitable at current AI costs, or add fair-use limits. *(/founder-cfo)*
- [ ] Name the acquisition channel and show the cost of a customer is paid back in a reasonable time. *(/founder-cfo, /founder-marketing)*
- [ ] Close the scheduler seam with a real hand-off to schedulers, or cut the calendar back to a saved-drafts list. *(founder, /founder-ops)*
- [ ] Lengthen the trial to cover at least one full content cycle (about a week). *(/founder-offer)*
- [ ] Build the offer around agency problems (client approvals, revisions, team handoffs), ship approvals and team seats, add an outcome guarantee, and test a higher price. *(/founder-offer, /founder-pricing)*
- [ ] Use a domain email rather than Gmail. *(/founder-brand)*
- [ ] Name an advantage that compounds, such as voice data that improves with use or an approval workflow agencies standardise on. *(founder)*

## Where the members disagree

The Product lens sees a well-focused product: one clear moment, sensible scope cuts, human review by design. It would fund it once the voice is proven. The Monopoly lens passes: even with great output there is nothing durable, so a scheduler adding the feature ends it. The Offers lens sits between them: the market and the angle are real, but at €19 it reads as a price play, so it wants the offer rebuilt around outcomes and a higher price.

## The strongest version the board can see

This differs from the pitch. The strongest Coast is not a cheap AI caption writer, but **the approval and voice layer between an agency and its clients**. The agency's team drafts in each client's voice, the client reviews and approves in Coast, and approved posts go straight to the agency's scheduler. That turns the "future" team seats and approvals into the core of the product. It gives Coast a reason to be paid more, such as per-client pricing or a higher Agency tier with an outcome guarantee. It also builds lock-in, because the client relationship and approval history live in Coast. Start with one tight segment the founder can reach directly, for example small agencies in Lithuania and the Baltics, prove the time saved with real numbers, then widen.
