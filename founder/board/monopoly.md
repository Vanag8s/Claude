# Monopoly lens
Score: 3/10 | Vote: PASS

## What is strong
- **A small market is named.** "Small agencies and freelancers running 3 to 50 brands, mainly in Europe" is the kind of narrow starting market the Monopoly lens wants. Small markets are easier to own.
- **The workflow hook is real.** Per-client voice profiles plus a per-client calendar fit how multi-brand managers actually work. Each saved voice profile is stored context, so switching to another tool costs the user something. That is the only seed of durability here.
- **Cheap to start.** Self-serve, euro pricing and no card on the trial mean the product can be tested quickly.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No proprietary edge, and a crowded market.** The core of the product is a brand brief turned into a prompt for a general AI model, wrapped in a chat window and a calendar. Nothing here is ten times better than the next option. A freelancer can get most of it from a general chat assistant with a saved brief. Schedulers and established AI writing tools, which already have distribution, can add or have already added "brand voice" as a feature. The site's comparison against unnamed "Others" avoids the real question: who else already sells this to the same buyer? Under the Monopoly lens this is competition, and competition destroys margin.
2. **No distribution story.** Nothing explains how the first 100 customers will arrive or what each will cost. Small agencies are spread out and price-sensitive, and €19 to €49 a month leaves little room to pay for acquiring them. Whether the testimonials and the "50+ brands" figure come from real customers is not stated, so there is no evidence of traction. A product with no channel is not a business.
3. **Unlimited AI on a flat fee, with no lock-in.** Every paid plan includes unlimited AI requests, and the per-request model cost is unknown. Heavy Agency users (unlimited clients at €99) could cost more than they pay. The plans are monthly and cancel-anytime, with no annual plan, no team seats and no approval workflows yet. Nothing ties a customer to Coast, so churn could be high. Neither economies of scale nor network effects are in sight.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- What is the secret? What do you know about multi-brand agencies that the schedulers and AI writing tools do not?
- Name the three products your buyer would otherwise use, and say where Coast is ten times better rather than slightly better.
- How do the first 100 paying customers arrive, and what does each one cost? (CAC is an open question.)
- What does one AI request cost you, and what does your heaviest Agency user cost per month?
- Are the testimonials and the "50+ brands" from real paying customers? How many paying users do you have today?
- What stops a copycat built on the same no-code platform and the same model in month six?
- Who is the team, and why are they the right people?
- Why now? What changed that makes this possible today but not two years ago?
- Which of network effects, scale, brand or proprietary data could Coast build over ten years? For example, could approval workflows or learned voice data become something competitors cannot copy?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
Not applicable (PASS). For a second look I would need:
- a narrow niche (one country, language or vertical) where Coast already has a large share of paying users
- a named, repeatable acquisition channel with a measured CAC
- the unit cost per AI request, plus a cap or pricing that protects margin
- one defensible element that is actually built, such as team approvals or proprietary voice data, rather than marked "future"
