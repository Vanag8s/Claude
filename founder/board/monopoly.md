# Monopoly lens
Score: 3/10 | Vote: PASS

## What is strong
- **A small market is named.** It is for social media managers and agencies who run several client brands, not "anyone who posts". That is the right shape for the Monopoly lens: a niche that could be owned first.
- **The positioning has a real wedge.** The site says "typical AI social tools [are] built for a single brand". Saving each client's voice (tone, audience, words to use and avoid) and switching between client workspaces is a concrete reason a multi-brand user might pick it.
- **A possible seed of lock-in.** Every client profile and calendar a user builds up makes leaving a little harder. If that store of voice data grew richer than a fresh prompt in a general AI tool, it could become know-how.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No durable advantage of any of the four kinds.** On proprietary technology, the core is AI text generation on a no-code builder (Bolt), and nothing shown is ten times better than pasting a saved brand brief into a general AI assistant. There are no network effects, since each account is private and users gain nothing from each other. There are no economies of scale; with unlimited requests, cost grows with usage. There is no brand yet: the contact is a Gmail address and the testimonials and claims are unverified. In month six, a copycat or an existing scheduler adding "client voice profiles" removes the reason to exist. *Answered by: competitor research.*
2. **Crowded ground and a missing step.** Coast does not publish, so the user still pays for and works in a separate scheduler. Any scheduler or agency tool that builds per-client AI drafting into the workflow already there owns the step after Coast and can take Coast's step too. Coast sits in the middle of someone else's workflow, which is the weakest place to defend. *Answered by: competitor research (who already offers multi-brand AI drafting inside a scheduler, and at what price).*
3. **No distribution, and pricing that may not pay for any.** The acquisition channel and its cost are not stated. At €19 to €99/month with a 2-day trial, there is little margin to pay for customer acquisition, and unlimited AI requests on every paid plan could turn heavy agency users into losses. The lens counts a product with no way to reach buyers as not yet a business. *Answered by: CFO unit economics (cost per request, cost to acquire a customer, payback) and a consumer panel (would multi-brand managers switch, and through which channel do they discover tools).*

## The questions I would ask the founder
- What is the secret: what do you know about multi-brand social work that the schedulers and general AI tools have missed?
- How do the first 100 paying customers arrive, through which channel, and at what cost each?
- What stops an existing scheduler from shipping per-client voice profiles in month six, and what would you still have that it lacks?
- Which part could become ten times better than the next option, measured how? The "10x faster" claim needs a source.
- Are the testimonials and the "50+ brands" figure from real users? How many users and paying customers are there today?
- What does one AI request cost you, and what does your heaviest "unlimited" user cost per month?
- Why now? What has changed that makes this the moment for a multi-brand tool?
- Can you be the clear default for one tight segment first (for example, freelancers in one country or one language), and how large is it?
- Who is the team, and how much time and money can you commit?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
Not applicable, because the vote is PASS. These would change it:
- Competitor research shows no strong multi-brand AI drafting tool within reach of the same buyer, or it names a clear segment Coast can lead.
- The founder names a believable path to an advantage that compounds, such as voice data that improves with use, a publishing or approval workflow agencies standardise on, or a channel competitors cannot copy cheaply.
- CFO unit economics show requests are affordable at the heaviest realistic use, or the plans are capped, and that the cost to acquire a customer is paid back within a reasonable period at current prices.
- Real, verifiable paying users replace the unverified testimonials.
