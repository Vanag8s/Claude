# Offers lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **The buyers are easy to find.** Small agencies and freelance social media managers gather in a few visible places, and every one of them handles several brands. A buyer who juggles 3 to 50 client voices is well defined.
- **Quick to a first result.** "5 min to set up a new client profile" and a chat that writes the first caption right away mean the buyer sees a result in the same session. That is the strongest part of the value equation.
- **The per-client voice is a real hook.** Keeping tone, audience and banned words separate for each client is a specific job a general chatbot does not do out of the box.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **Nothing proves it works.** The "10x faster" and "50+ brands" stats and the three testimonials have no stated source. Under this lens, proof is the most common reason an offer fails. If those testimonials are not real customers, the likelihood score drops to zero and the brand takes a trust hit with the very buyers it needs to win over.
2. **It is a commodity priced as one.** At €19 to €49, the offer is about price and convenience, not outcome. A freelancer can copy most of it with a general AI assistant set up with a saved brief per client, at little or no extra cost. Nothing on the site would make a buyer feel stupid saying no: no guarantee, no outcome promise, no named competitor it beats.
3. **Small market, weak economics.** These buyers have thin budgets, and the pain (writing captions) is real but not massive. "Unlimited AI requests" at a flat price, with an unknown cost per request, means the heaviest users (the Agency tier, unlimited clients) could cost more than they pay. It is also unknown whether this market is growing or being squeezed by free AI tools.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- How many paying users do you have, and are the testimonials and stats from real customers? If not, when will you take them down?
- What does one AI request cost you, and what does a heavy Agency user cost per month?
- Why would a freelancer pay you rather than keep a saved brief in a general AI assistant?
- What outcome does the buyer get, in their own words: hours saved, more clients per manager, fewer revisions from clients?
- Why a 2-day trial? Can a buyer see client approval of a post within two days?
- Could you double Pro to €99 and still be the obvious choice? If not, what is missing?
- What is the size of the European small-agency market, and what does it cost to acquire one paying user? (Both unknown today.)

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. Replace every unverified stat and testimonial with real ones, or remove them, before spending anything on marketing.
2. Show the AI cost per request, then put a fair-use cap on "unlimited" or price it so every tier has a positive margin.
3. Rebuild the offer around an outcome rather than a feature list: for example "a month of on-brand content for a new client in under an hour", backed by a guarantee (refund if the client rejects the voice in the first 30 days).
4. Build the stack around the buyer's real blockers: client approval links, publishing or scheduler export, and onboarding for brand briefs. Approvals are listed as "future" but sit right on the path to the outcome. Then test a higher price.
5. Show 10 or more paying agencies who are still subscribed after 60 days.
