# Offers lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **The market can be named and reached.** Social media agencies and freelance managers who run several client brands are a defined group, and they gather in places that can be found online. The pain is real: writing in many brand voices, every week, under deadline. Whether the market is growing and how much buyers spend is not in the brief (competitor research).
- **Fast time to first result.** "Add a client (5 minutes), brief the assistant, save to calendar" is a short path to a first draft. That scores well on the time part of the value equation.
- **A clear angle.** "Built for agencies managing multiple brands" rather than for one brand gives the offer a reason to exist beyond being another AI writer. Saved per-client voice (tone, audience, words to use and avoid) is the core of that angle.
- **Low entry price and a no-card trial** make the first yes easy.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **Nothing proves it works, and the proof shown could backfire.** Likelihood is the weakest part of the value equation here. The page's claims ("10x faster", "50+ brands") and the three testimonials are unverified, per the brief. If they are not from real customers, they hurt trust with exactly the buyers who judge marketing copy for a living. There is no guarantee to make up for the missing proof.
2. **It is close to a commodity.** Buyers can already get captions and scripts from general AI chat tools and other AI social tools (who these are and what they charge needs competitor research). Per-client voice and a tracking calendar may not be enough to make the buyer feel stupid saying no, especially when €19 makes the purchase look like a price play. Prices from €19 to €99 suggest it competes on being cheap, not on outcome.
3. **The offer is unlimited, but the cost per request is unknown.** Every paid plan includes unlimited AI requests, and Agency also has unlimited client profiles. The AI/API cost per request is unknown, so heavy users on the top plan could cost more than they pay (CFO unit economics). This is a margin risk in the stack itself, not just a pricing detail.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- How many users and paying customers do you have, and are the testimonials and the "10x" and "50+ brands" figures from them? (Founder; consumer panel to test whether buyers believe the claims.)
- What does an agency do today instead, and what does that cost them in hours or money? (Consumer panel; competitor research.)
- Which outcome do agencies pay for: faster drafts, fewer client revisions, or more clients per manager? Can you measure it for one customer? (Consumer panel.)
- What does an average and a heavy Agency-plan user cost you per month in AI calls? (CFO unit economics.)
- Could you double the price and still be the obvious choice? What would have to be added? (Competitor research; consumer panel.)
- Why is the trial 2 days, when an agency needs a full content cycle to see the value? (Consumer panel.)
- How do the first 100 customers find you, and what does each cost? (Founder; CFO unit economics.)

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **Real proof replaces unverified proof.** Remove any testimonial or figure that is not from a real customer. Get at least a handful of named agency users with a measured before-and-after (for example, hours per client per week).
2. **Unit economics hold on the unlimited promise.** CFO unit economics shows AI cost per heavy user stays well under plan price, or the plans get fair-use limits.
3. **Build the stack around the agency's real problems, then price on outcome.** List the obstacles between brief and published post: client approvals, revisions, team handoffs, getting content into the scheduler. Turn the cheap-to-deliver ones into included solutions. The approvals and team seats now marked "future" are the obvious first ones. Then add a guarantee tied to the outcome, such as time saved in the first month or your money back, and test a higher price.
4. **Trial long enough to reach the result.** Test a trial that covers at least one full content cycle for a real client.
