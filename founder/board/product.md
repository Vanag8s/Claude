# Product lens
Score: 5 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **One clear moment.** An agency manager switches to a client, briefs the chat, and gets a draft in that client's saved voice (tone, audience, words to use and avoid). That is a real job, and it is easy to picture.
- **A focused wedge.** "Built for agencies managing multiple brands", not one brand. The per-client voice profile and per-client workspace are what the product is built around, which is the right one thing to be brilliant at.
- **Sensible scope cuts already made.** It does not publish to social platforms, and team seats and approvals are marked as future work. That is saying no, and it keeps the surface small.
- **Human in the loop by design.** The AI writes the first draft and a person reviews it. That fits how agencies have to answer to their clients.
- **The headline fits in one line**: "Coast through your content. Every client, every platform."

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **The output is no better than a generic chatbot with a saved prompt.** The whole product rests on the voice profile making drafts that clearly sound like each client. If a manager can get the same result by pasting a brand brief into a general AI tool, there is nothing left to pay for. Nothing in the brief shows the output quality. Open question, answered by **competitor research** (side-by-side drafts against general AI tools and AI social tools) and a **consumer panel** (can agency managers tell which drafts are on-voice?).
2. **The seam between Coast and the scheduler.** Users copy content out and paste it into another tool, then keep Coast's draft/scheduled/published status up to date by hand. Statuses will drift, and the calendar stops being trusted. So the "per-client calendar" turns into a second calendar to maintain. Open question: do target users already live in a scheduler that has its own calendar? Answered by the **consumer panel**.
3. **The touchpoints around the product undercut the craft.** A Gmail contact address, testimonials and figures ("10x faster", "50+ brands") that the founder has not confirmed are real, and a 2-day trial. Agencies need about a week of real client work to judge voice quality, and 2 days barely covers setting up a few clients. Every one of these touchpoints is part of the product. If one claim turns out to be unsupported, buyers will doubt the rest of it.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- In one sentence: what does Coast do that a general AI chat with a saved brand prompt does not?
- Walk me through the first five minutes: sign-up, first client profile, first draft. What does the user see, and when do they get their first "this sounds like my client" moment?
- How does a draft get from Coast into the scheduler, and how does the status in Coast get updated after that? Who does it, and how often does it go stale?
- Are the testimonials and the "10x" and "50+ brands" figures from real users? If not, when will they come down?
- How many people are using it now, and how many pay? (Unknown.)
- What does an AI request cost you, given that every paid plan has unlimited requests? (Unknown. Answered by **CFO unit economics**.)
- Why 2 days for the trial? Is that long enough for an agency to test the voice on real clients?
- If you had to cut one feature, the chat or the calendar, which would it be?
- How do the first customers find Coast? (Acquisition channel not stated.)

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **Prove the voice.** Run a blind test of Coast drafts against general AI tools on real client briefs (via the **consumer panel** and **competitor research**). Coast must win clearly.
2. **Fix the trust touchpoints.** Remove or verify every testimonial and figure, use a domain email, and lengthen the trial to cover real client work.
3. **Close or shrink the scheduler seam.** Either show that users keep calendar statuses accurate, or cut the calendar back to a simple saved-drafts list until there is a real hand-off to schedulers.
4. **Make unlimited requests safe.** **CFO unit economics** must show that heavy users on each plan stay profitable at current AI costs.
