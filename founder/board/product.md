# Product lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **The one-sentence pitch mostly works:** "Captions, ideas and scripts in each client's voice, for people who run many brands." The per-client voice profile is the right thing to build around, because generic AI tools don't remember a voice from one client to the next.
- **The first five minutes are short:** add a client, brief it in chat, save to the calendar. Three steps, with no templates to learn. A self-serve trial with no card is the right kind of welcome.
- **There is some discipline about scope:** no direct publishing and no image or video generation. Saying no to those is a defensible way to focus, if it is a deliberate choice.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **The seam sits exactly where the job ends.** The customer's job ends when the post is published and the client has signed it off. Coast stops before both. Users copy text into a separate scheduler, and approvals are listed as "future". So the agency's real workflow (draft, client approval, schedule, publish) is spread across other tools, with Coast as one extra tab. A content calendar that is ticked "scheduled/published" by hand will go stale within weeks. Once that happens, the "one workspace" promise is broken.
2. **Craft at the touchpoints undercuts trust.** The app runs on a Bolt subdomain with "dvai-rebranding" in the URL and a Gmail contact address. The stats ("10x faster", "50+ brands") and the three testimonials have no stated source. Agencies are selling taste to their own clients, and these are the details they notice. A business that looks unfinished where buyers can see it raises the question of what it skips where they cannot: data isolation, uptime, output quality. If the testimonials or stats are not real, that is a fatal flaw, not a cosmetic one.
3. **"In each client's voice" is the whole product, and it is unproven.** Nothing shows that output from a 5-minute brief actually sounds like the client, rather than like generic AI text with the tone dial moved. If an agency still rewrites most drafts, the "10x" claim falls apart and Coast becomes a commodity. A 2-day trial is also too short for a manager to see that voice hold up across a week of real posts.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- What is the one thing? Is it voice fidelity, the multi-client workspace or the calendar? If you had to cut two, which survives?
- Walk me through a real agency's week in Coast. Where does the text leave Coast, and what goes wrong at that hand-off?
- What share of generated drafts get published with little or no editing? How do you measure that?
- Where do the "10x" and "50+ brands" figures come from? Are the three testimonials paying customers?
- Why 2 days for the trial? What do you expect a user to have experienced by the end of it?
- How many users and paying accounts do you have today, and what is the trial-to-paid rate? (Unknown.)
- What is the cost per AI request, and does "unlimited" survive a heavy Agency-plan user? (Unknown.)
- When will approvals and team seats ship, given that agencies, the core buyer, need them?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **Prove voice fidelity.** Show measured evidence from real agencies, such as edit rate or share of drafts published as-is, that output from a profile reads as that client.
2. **Clean up every visible touchpoint.** Use your own domain and a business email. Either source the stats and testimonials or remove them.
3. **Close the main seam.** Ship either client approvals or a real hand-off to the scheduler (an export or integration), so the calendar shows the truth without manual ticking.
4. **Cut the roadmap to that one job.** Add nothing new until conditions 1 to 3 are met.
